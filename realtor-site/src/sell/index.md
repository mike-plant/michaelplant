Before we talk about price, I walk your house the way a buyer's inspector will. Then you get a short, honest list: fix these, leave these, and what each one is likely to do to your price.

## Why start there

Most of what scares buyers in an older house is predictable. Water in the basement, an old electrical panel, a roof near the end, a porch that's tired. Some of it is worth fixing before you list. A lot of it isn't. The expensive mistake is guessing wrong in either direction: spending $30,000 on a kitchen a buyer was going to redo anyway, or skipping a $600 repair that costs you $6,000 in a negotiation.

I've been working on houses like yours since 1992. That's the part I bring.

## What I look at

- Roof, gutters, and box gutters
- Basement water, foundation, and grading
- Electrical panel and any knob and tube wiring
- Plumbing, including old galvanized lines
- Furnace, water heater, and their ages
- Windows, porches, and exterior wood
- The small stuff a buyer notices in the first five minutes

## How it works

<ol class="steps">

## If something needs fixing

I'll point you to people I trust. I also own Reclaim, a small home repair business that does exterior carpentry like porches, trim, and rot. If Reclaim could do a repair, I'll tell you that it's my company, and you're never expected to use it.
