Chapter 2

The one offer

Marie ends everything with one clear action. Mine is the walkthrough.

I'll walk your house and tell you what I'd fix, what I'd leave alone, and what it's likely to cost. Text me.Fix or Leave It walkthrough · free · about 30 minutes

One offer, three outcomes
  • Staying put: it becomes a Reclaim job, or a referral to a trade I trust.
  • Selling soon: it becomes the pre-listing walkthrough on michaelplant.realtor/sell.
  • Buying: I walk the house they're looking at.
Rules for it
  • When it might turn into a Reclaim job, say it's my company, in writing, and name two other contractors. NAR Standard 6-1 requires disclosure at the time of the recommendation.
  • If price comes up, call it a "price opinion" or "market analysis," never an appraisal.
  • It ends every post, every campaign page, and every Reclaim invoice.

How I'll run it
  • Start with $300 to $600 a month, split between Local Services Ads and a Meta retargeting test.
  • Judge on jobs booked and walkthroughs, not leads. Track cost per booked job.
  • Answer every lead within 5 minutes during work hours. The industry average is about 90 minutes, and the fast responder usually wins.
  • Ads point at a campaign page (see Where I send people), never at the brand pages.
  • Free now, paid later: install GA4 and the Meta Pixel today and build audiences: site visitors, video viewers, people who tapped call or text. Building costs nothing. Ads need about 100 people in an audience, so by the time you spend $10 a day, the warm audience is already there.

Before the first dollar

  • Reclaim's own site with one page per service.
  • Reclaim's Google profile verified, with the first few reviews.
  • A consent line on any ad form, so texting leads back is allowed.
Chapter 5

Being found

I won't beat Zillow for "Lakewood homes for sale." I can win the map for "realtor Lakewood" and "porch repair Lakewood," plus specific questions.

Agent Google profile
  • Name: "Michael Plant." Google doesn't allow the brokerage in the name at a multi-agent office.
  • Category: Real estate agent.
  • Address: not the Red 1 office, where it gets filtered. Use a real place I work from, set as a service area with the address hidden, unless I actually meet people there during posted hours.
  • Red 1 Realty in the description. Website: michaelplant.realtor, which shows the brokerage on every page. Confirm with Red 1 (the Ask Red 1 chapter).
Reclaim Google profile
  • Primary category Carpenter, with Handyman and others as secondary. Pick Google's predefined services too.
  • Service area. Show the address only if customers come there. Hiding it costs some ranking, but showing a place nobody can visit breaks the rules.
  • Expect video verification: one continuous video showing tools or truck and a business document.

The bookstore basement office: it's a real place I work from, so it can be Reclaim's hidden service-area base. For the agent profile, Google expects me reachable there during posted hours, and a basement with no public entrance is a weak fit. A suite number only counts if it's real on the building and gets mail; an invented one can get the profile removed. Ohio has no rule I could find against listing a personal office, but presenting it as a real estate office could look like an unlicensed branch. Ask Red 1, and check with Emily first since it's her building too.

Reviews: aim for 10 to 20 Google reviews with written comments on each profile, then a steady trickle. Rankings climb fast up to about that point and then flatten, and 74% of people want reviews from the last 3 months. Hand a review QR card at every closing and finished job. Ask everyone, not only the happy ones; Google bans gating and incentives. Reply to every review. On Yelp, claim the listing and never ask for reviews.

Chapter 6

The Google engine for real estate

From Brian Icenhower's lead-source ranking, checked against the research. His big point holds: people who don't know an agent trust Google, and the agent profile plus Local Services Ads bring in sellers as well as buyers. Some of his specifics didn't hold up, and those are marked.

SourceWhat it costsFit for me
People I knowTimeAlways first. Icenhower's own rule: online leads are extra income, not a replacement.
Google Business ProfileFreeBuild now. Reviews and services are what move it.
Google Local Services Ads, real estatePay per valid lead. One vendor says $24 to $36. Message leads cost half.Test once the profile has reviews. Pick the seller's agent service type to match the walkthrough offer.
Pay-at-close referral networks25 to 40% of the commission at closing. No cost upfront.Realtor.com and HomeLight are the likeliest to take a part-time agent. Zillow, Redfin, and UpNest require production history. Needs Red 1 approval.

The agent profile, set up right
  • Services: add every one that's true, with a sentence each: Seller's agent, Buyer's agent, Pre-listing walkthrough, Price opinion, Older homes. A Sterling Sky test found adding services moved rankings.
  • Reviews are the strongest signal: 10 to 20 with written comments, then steady. Use michaelplant.realtor/review on every card and text.
  • Booking link goes to michaelplant.realtor/sell (the walkthrough).
  • Exact same name and phone as the site and every other listing. He's right that mismatches confuse Google.
  • Photos: you, real houses you've helped with (with permission), Lakewood streets.
Where he's wrong or unproven
  • "Post regularly or AI won't find you." A Sterling Sky test found weekly posts didn't change map rankings. Post anyway when you have something real; it shows you're active. Just don't expect posts to do the ranking work.
  • "75% of business comes after the seventh follow-up." No study behind it. Speed is better proven: leads contacted within an hour were about 7 times more likely to qualify (Harvard Business Review, 2011).
  • Read AI "$39 a month." The paid note-takers run about $10 to $20 a month: Fireflies, Otter, Read AI.
  • YouTube helps local ranking. No evidence for that. YouTube is cited more and more in AI answers for how-to topics, though, so posting your Fix or Leave It videos there as Shorts is cheap upside.

Local Services Ads for real estate, the facts:

  • Category: Google's US page lists "Real estate agents and brokers" and includes Ohio. Confirm in the sign-up flow with a Lakewood ZIP code.
  • Requirements: a license check and background checks. Your Red 1 details must match your state license and your Google profile.
  • Budget and credits: you set a weekly budget. Google now credits invalid leads automatically instead of letting you dispute them.
  • The badge: it's now "Google Verified." The old money-back guarantee ended October 20, 2025.

Nobody publishes the buyer and seller mix or conversion rates, so track your own with the call and text tagging that's already built.

The referral networks also give AI tools more places to find you. One of Icenhower's agents noticed his brokerage showing up in more search results after he filled out these profiles. That's an anecdote, but it lines up with Whitespark's top AI factor: being prominent on industry sites. Fill out every profile you qualify for, with the same name, photo, and phone.

Do, strongest evidence first
  • Both Google profiles, complete and current.
  • Allow the search crawlers on both sites: Googlebot, Bingbot, OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Claude-SearchBot, Applebot.
  • Claim Yelp, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect.

Measure it like an analyst
  • Answers change a lot between runs; the same question repeated cites the same sources only about 43% of the time. One check means nothing.
  • Keep a fixed set of 10 prompts, like "who should I call for porch repair in Lakewood Ohio" and "best realtor for old houses in Lakewood." Run each in ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Perplexity, and Copilot once a month. Log whether I'm named and which sources are cited.
  • Bing Webmaster Tools' AI Performance report covers Copilot citations.
  • Skip llms.txt. No major AI search engine has said it uses it.

The rules
  • Real estate on ChatGPT: no ads for specific homes for sale or rent. Advertise you and the walkthrough, never an address or listing.
  • Real estate on Google and Microsoft: no ZIP, age, gender, parental, or marital targeting. Use a radius, city, county, or metro area. On ChatGPT, use the Cleveland metro area to stay safe.
  • Reclaim isn't housing on Google or ChatGPT, so ZIP targeting is fine there. Meta still treats it as housing.
  • Every ad shows Michael Plant | Red 1 Realty at equal size when it's real estate. The campaign page does too.
  • Avoid anyone selling a guaranteed ChatGPT ranking. There's no paid organic placement.
The test, about $300 to $600 a month
  • Google Local Services Ads for Reclaim first. Proven, pay per lead.
  • ChatGPT, $100 to $150 a month for 2 months, Reclaim first. Target Lakewood-area ZIPs and send people to reclaimhomerepair.com/go/porch?utm_source=chatgpt. Then real estate: the Cleveland metro area, sent to michaelplant.realtor/go/walkthrough?utm_source=chatgpt.
  • Microsoft Advertising, about $50 a month: import the Google campaigns and add a logo so they can show in Copilot.
  • Measure clicks to calls and texts, not ChatGPT's reported clicks. Agencies found OpenAI's click counts didn't match Google Analytics. cta.js already records the utm_source on every call and text tap.

Early results, honestly:

  • ChatGPT ads are getting clicked less than search ads. Average click-through is about 0.6 to 0.7%, versus about 2% on search.
  • One agency spent $415 at about $7 a click and got no buyers.
  • Visitors who come from AI assistants convert well once they arrive, about 1.5 times other referrals in one retail study.

That's why this is a capped test. The upside is learning the channel while it's cheap and empty of local competitors.

Chapter 10

The AI desk

Where I can actually get ahead. Most agents use a blank ChatGPT. Mine knows my voice, my rules, and my jobs: Google posts, review replies, follow-up texts from call notes, Fix or Leave It, YouTube scripts, and the monthly AI check.

Setup, 10 minutes: claude.ai, then Projects, then New project. Paste the instructions below. Add this Playbook, the Brand Book, and the Field Kit as project files.

Follow-up workflow:

  1. Take the call on speaker with a note-taker running (Fireflies, Otter, or Read AI, about $10 to $20 a month).
  2. Paste the transcript into the project.
  3. You get a summary, three texts for 2, 4, and 6 weeks out, and the next step.
  4. Log the next step in HubSpot and send the texts yourself.

Recording: Ohio only needs your consent to record a call. But if the person is in a state like California, its all-party rule can still apply. When a caller might be out of state, say "I record my calls so I don't forget anything" at the start.

Texting: texts you type and send yourself are low risk. Texts a platform sends from a list need prior consent.
