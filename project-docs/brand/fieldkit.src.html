Every job, 5 minutes

Chapter 2

Photos with a phone

You don't need a camera. You need the same spot, good light, and a clean frame.

Do
  • Wipe the lens on your shirt. Job-site phones are always smudged.
  • Turn on the grid (iPhone: Settings, Camera, Grid) and keep lines straight.
  • Stand in the same spot for before and after. Note a landmark: the downspout, the second step.
  • Phone at chest height, level, not tilted up or down.
  • Tap and hold on the subject to lock focus and brightness.
  • Use the 1x lens. Walk closer instead of zooming. 0.5x only in tight spaces; it bends straight lines.
  • Overcast days are best. In full sun, shoot with the sun behind you.
  • Put a hand, tool, or tape measure in the close-ups for scale.
  • Horizontal for before and afters on the website. Vertical for social.
Don't
  • Leave trash, cords, or buckets in the frame. Ten seconds of moving stuff is worth it.
  • Show house numbers, license plates, or kids.
  • Use filters. Real photos are the point.
  • Shoot into a bright window or the sky.
  • Post a client's house without asking.
Chapter 3

Video

Short, vertical, one idea. You talking beats music over clips.

The shape
  • Seconds 0 to 3: show the problem and say it. "This column base is rotted through."
  • Middle: what caused it and what you're doing about it.
  • End: your take. "Fix it. Here's why." Or "Leave it."
  • 15 to 45 seconds total. One idea per video.
The gear
  • Vertical, 1x lens, phone propped at chest height or held at arm's length.
  • Get out of the wind or use a clip-on mic. Bad sound kills more videos than bad picture.
  • Hold each shot for 3 seconds before moving.
  • Add captions in Instagram's editor. Most people watch with sound off.
  • Hands-working clips with no talking are fine as extra footage.
Chapter 4

Talk it, don't write it

Record a voice memo in the truck before you drive off. Your phone's Voice Memos app makes a transcript. Paste it into Claude with the prompt below and you get a caption, a group post, and an article draft in your voice.

Answer these four out loud, in about 2 minutes:

  1. What did we find?
  2. What do most people do, or get wrong, about this?
  3. What would I do, and why? Fix it, or leave it?
  4. What did it take: time, money, materials? When should someone call a pro?
I'm Mike Plant, an old-house guy in Lakewood, Ohio. I run Houses of Lakewood and Reclaim Home Repair. Below is a voice memo transcript from a job. Chapter 5

The Fix or Leave It shape

"Article" just means the longer version of a post. Same six parts every time, so you never start from a blank page.

  1. The thing. One photo, one sentence. "This is a box gutter."
  2. Why it happens here. What it is about 100-year-old Lakewood houses that causes it.
  3. The verdict. Fix it, or leave it. Say it plainly.
  4. What I'd do. The repair, or what to watch instead.
  5. What it takes. Time, a price range if you know it, materials.
  6. When to call someone. And who: an electrician, a roofer, me.

Repair topics live on Reclaim's site. Buying and selling topics live on michaelplant.realtor. Everything starts as a post in the group.

Chapter 6

This week's jobs

Shot lists for what you're already doing. The opinions are yours to fill in.

Insulation at your buddy's
  • Shots: the empty attic or wall cavity, what was there before (old insulation, nothing, newspapers), any wiring you found, air sealing in progress, the finished depth with a tape measure in frame.
  • Video: "Here's what's in the attic of a 100-year-old Lakewood house."
  • Fix or Leave It ideas: old wiring in the attic (if you see knob and tube, that's an electrician's call before anything gets covered), air sealing before insulation, what you'd skip.
  • Caption angle: "Helping my buddy with his attic today. Here's what we found."
Framing at Leslie's
  • Shots: wide of the open wall or floor, the joints, any notched or cut joists, old repairs, a hand or tape for scale, the old lumber's rough-sawn size next to modern lumber if you have it.
  • Video: "What 100-year-old framing tells you."
  • Fix or Leave It ideas: a joist someone notched for a pipe, an old repair that's actually fine, what you'd sister and why.
  • Ask Leslie first.
