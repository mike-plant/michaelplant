Chapter 3

The look

Taken from the brand kit and already live on the site branch. This is the whole system. Copy it, don't extend it.

Paper#EFE8DAEvery background.
Ink#1F1C18Text, rules, portrait. Never pure black.
Ink soft#4A443BSecondary text.
Faint#857B6DLabels and fine print.

No accent color. If something needs to stand out, make it bigger or put it in a box, don't color it.

Michael Plant

Cormorant SC, weight 600. Names and headings. Letter-spacing about 0.04em. Name always on one line.

Before you spend $12,000 on new windows, here's what I'd look at first.

EB Garamond, 400 and 500, italic for asides. Everything else. Left-aligned.

Always

  • One single ink frame, 1 to 1.5px.
  • Engraved portrait for designed things. Real photo for avatars.
  • Real photos and phone video for everyday posts. Unfiltered is fine.
  • The same avatar photo on every platform.
  • One line of type per graphic. Two at most.

Never

  • Double borders, wide-tracked caps everywhere, centered paragraphs. That's wedding stationery.
  • Emoji, icons, stock photos, house-key clip art.
  • A new logo. The portrait is the logo.
  • "Just Listed" graphics in this style. Listings go on brokerage templates.
  • The original AI card portrait. Its face is wrong.
Chapter 4

The voice

Write like Mike texting someone he just met. Plain, warm, a little funny, short sentences.

  • Hey, I'm Mike.
  • Mostly, I just want to know the people around me better. If I can help, connect you with someone, or you just want to grab coffee, reach out.
  • Want to grab coffee? Seriously. Text me.
  • Loving houses since 1992. (sparingly; it opens the story about your dad)

Sounds like Mike

  • "Here's what I'd do."
  • "I'd leave this alone."
  • "That took longer than it should have."
  • "I don't know yet. I'll tell you when I do."
  • Real numbers: "$14,000 for nine windows."

Doesn't

  • Trusted, premier, your local expert, dream home.
  • "Are you thinking of selling?" as an opener.
  • Adjective stacks. Hashtag walls.
  • Em or en dashes. Use commas, colons, periods.
  • Telling people who a neighborhood is "for."
Chapter 5

What I publish

Four kinds of posts, four recurring series, one weekly rhythm. Recurring series are the cheapest trick Marie uses: people learn what to expect, and you never start from a blank page.

The four kinds
  • Something I'm doing. Your own projects, Reclaim jobs, helping a friend.
  • Something I noticed. Patterns you see in Lakewood houses.
  • Something people should know. Before you spend money, here's my take.
  • Life. Family, the bookstore, neighbors, Lakewood events. About 1 in 4 posts.
The weekly rhythm
  • 3 posts a week. One useful, one from the job, one from life.
  • Sunday, 45 minutes. Write the three captions from this week's photos.
  • On every job: 3 photos and one 15 second vertical video before you leave. That is the whole content pipeline.
  • Post once, share twice. Instagram first, then the Facebook group, then your personal Facebook if it fits.
Fix or Leave ItSomething people should know

One photo of one thing in an old house. Your verdict and why, in under 120 words. The series that will get saved and shared.

Quote CheckSomething people should know

A real contractor quote, names removed. What's normal, what you'd ask, what you'd push back on. Nobody else in Lakewood can do this honestly.

Built 19__Something I noticed

A Lakewood house or street and what its year tells you: how it was framed, what's likely behind the plaster, who lived there. The Houses of Lakewood group already loves this.

On the JobSomething I'm doing

Short video from today's work. What you're doing, the part that went sideways, what you'd tell a homeowner doing it themselves.

Where it goes. Facebook group first in spirit, because that is where Lakewood homeowners already are, and you own the room. Instagram for reach and as the portfolio people check before they text you. Skip TikTok, YouTube, and LinkedIn content for now. LinkedIn gets a profile refresh and nothing else.

What counts as working. Not followers. Count texts and DMs that mention a post, group joins, saves and shares, card scans, and the "how did you hear about me" answer in HubSpot. Write them in a note each Sunday. That's the scoreboard.

Chapter 9

Guardrails

My reading, not legal advice. Red 1's broker supervises your advertising and has the final say.

SituationWhat to do
Post offers real estate helpOhio (OAC 1301:5-1-02) counts social posts as advertising. Name Red 1 Realty in the post at the same prominence as your name. A Fix or Leave It post that doesn't offer services doesn't need it.
Instagram bioKeep real estate out of the bio and let the link route it, or name Red 1 Realty in the bio. Ask the broker which they prefer, once.
Someone else's listingDon't post it, or its interior, without the listing agent's permission.
Talking about neighborhoodsDescribe houses, streets, lots, walkability, prices. Never who a place is "for" or who lives there. Fair Housing.
Client storiesAsk permission. Use first names only if they say yes. No sale prices without consent.
House engravingsClosing gifts. If posted with any sales language, add the brokerage.
Quote CheckRemove contractor names and addresses. The point is teaching, not shaming a business.
Lead paintPre-1978 houses (nearly all of Lakewood). On camera, show lead-safe work or say why you're not.
